Late-Night Snackers and Delivery-Only Users occupy the middle-to-high price sensitivity range. The delivery platform ecosystem emerges as the decisive factor determining whether price-sensitive guests substitute within I'M Kazakhstan or migrate to competitors entirely. Second, their preference for premium menu items (Pizza, Burgers, Desserts for Big Spender; Burgers and Pasta for Loyal Regular) means they absorb the full price increase without downward substitution. This creates temporal volatility—the same guest exhibits different behaviors across different occasions, making revenue forecasting difficult. Delivery-Only User, possessing "a price sensitivity of 0.7 and a loyalty of 0.4," demonstrates similar middle-volatility characteristics; their moderate price sensitivity combined with low loyalty means they remain vulnerable to platform-driven competitor promotion.

Family-oriented segments including Family with Kids (price sensitivity 0.6, loyalty 0.5) and Family Guest (price sensitivity 0.6) occupy a critical middle position where budget constraints force trade-offs. These segments do not defect entirely but rather fragment their spending across different visit types—some visits focused on premium items, others on budget alternatives. This creates revenue volatility at the transaction level; the restaurant cannot predict whether a family visit will generate a high-value pizza-and-desserts order or a low-value sides-and-drinks purchase.

**Structural Divergence: Revenue Concentration and Margin Vulnerability**

The bifurcation creates a structural dependency where I'M Kazakhstan's revenue increasingly concentrates among a small premium segment while mass-market segments become progressively less reliable. This concentration manifests in three critical vulnerabilities:

First, revenue stability becomes dependent on premium segment retention. If Big Spender or Loyal Regular segments experience even modest defection (e.g., 5-10% churn), the aggregate margin expansion (+1.14%) evaporates entirely. The premium tier's high transaction values mean each defection represents disproportionate revenue loss. Conversely, a 20% defection rate among Budget Student or Price-First Commuter segments produces minimal aggregate revenue impact because their low transaction values mean their collective contribution, despite larger population size, remains modest.

Second, the volatility tier's fragmentation creates operational complexity and margin pressure. Substitution patterns (Budget Students shifting from burgers to sides) change the product mix toward lower-margin items. Frequency reduction among Late-Night Snackers and Delivery-Only Users reduces transaction volume even if per-transaction margins remain stable. Defection to competitors among First-Time Visitors and extreme price-sensitive segments eliminates the possibility of future loyalty formation—these guests never develop the attachment that would make them part of the premium stability tier.

Third, the bifurcation creates asymmetric competitive vulnerability. Premium segments show minimal competitor susceptibility; Big Spender's low price sensitivity (0.1) means competitor price promotions produce negligible defection. Mass-market segments, conversely, demonstrate extreme competitor vulnerability; Budget Student's price sensitivity of 0.9 means competitor price advantages trigger immediate switching. This asymmetry means I'M Kazakhstan's competitive position weakens precisely in the segments where volume and market share matter most.

**Future Trajectory: Deepening Bifurcation and Market Concentration Risk**

The simulation indicates that the bifurcation will deepen over time as mass-market segments progressively fragment and premium segments consolidate. As budget-conscious guests experience repeated price increases or encounter competitor alternatives through delivery platforms, their loyalty scores decline further, accelerating defection. Simultaneously, the premium segment's stable revenue contribution makes them increasingly central to restaurant operations; marketing, menu development, and operational decisions increasingly optimize for this high-loyalty, low-price-sensitivity cohort.

This deepening bifurcation creates a critical strategic inflection point. I'M Kazakhstan faces a choice between pursuing premium market concentration (optimizing for Big Spender and Loyal Regular segments, accepting mass-market defection) or attempting to retain mass-market volume through alternative retention mechanisms (delivery platform optimization, targeted promotions, menu innovation). The research objective explicitly identifies that "the research goal is to understand which guests accept the I'M Kazakhstan menu price change quietly, which complain, which substitute a cheaper or similar item, and which switch to a competitor"—and the simulation reveals that this understanding maps directly onto a bifurcated market where premium acceptance and mass-market volatility represent two fundamentally different business models competing for dominance within the same restaurant chain.
